[[TLDR:closed]]
Large Ising problems outgrow one device and expose three system costs: coupling data move through the memory hierarchy, colored Gibbs updates synchronize, and boundary states cross device links. We separate these costs in a performance model calibrated across GPUs, FPGAs, and ASICs. Our roofline analysis shows that Ising workloads have low arithmetic intensity, and our measurements show that performance drops sharply when the working set spills beyond on-chip storage. We develop two systematic approaches to system-level acceleration. Bounded-staleness sampling reduces synchronization frequency in two ways: multi-rate bounded staleness delays boundary-state propagation to interior p-bits within a partition, while cross-partition bounded staleness delays state propagation across a partition boundary. A phase-offset pipeline places boundary exchange behind interior computation. A fabricated multi-chip ASIC implements the streaming schedule.
