[[TLDR:closed]]
Large Ising problems outgrow one device. Once the machine is distributed, coupling data move through a memory hierarchy, colored Gibbs updates synchronize, and boundary states cross device links. We model those costs separately and calibrate the model across GPUs, FPGAs, and ASICs. Our roofline analysis finds that Ising workloads have low arithmetic intensity, and our measurements show a sharp performance loss when the working set spills beyond on-chip storage. We then develop two system-level acceleration approaches. Bounded-staleness sampling reduces synchronization frequency through multi-rate bounded staleness within a partition or cross-partition bounded staleness across a partition boundary. Phase-offset pipelining overlaps boundary exchange with interior computation. The streaming schedule is implemented on a fabricated multi-chip ASIC.
