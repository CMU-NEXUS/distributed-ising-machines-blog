## Two forms of bounded-staleness sampling

Colored Gibbs sampling places a barrier between color steps so every update sees fresh committed neighbor states. At small problem sizes, synchronization can take more time than the useful work. We consider two bounded-staleness mechanisms that reduce synchronization frequency when the workload tolerates stale neighbor observations.

**Multi-rate bounded staleness** delays boundary-state propagation to interior p-bits. An interior p-bit can therefore observe a stale boundary-neighbor state on a boundary--interior interaction within its partition. **Cross-partition bounded staleness** delays state propagation across a partition boundary. A boundary p-bit can therefore observe a stale neighbor state from the adjacent partition.

Both mechanisms use the same bound, **F**, on the age of an observed neighbor state. **F = 1** is the fully synchronized baseline. The distinction is the edge set to which the bound applies: boundary--interior edges for multi-rate bounded staleness and partition-crossing edges for cross-partition bounded staleness.

Both controls create an interior optimum. Reducing synchronization frequency first cuts sweep time faster than it lowers success probability. Past the optimum, quality loss dominates and time-to-solution rises again.

The graph determines the useful range. Sparse partitions expose a small boundary relative to the interior, so fewer p-bits observe stale neighbors. Dense graphs spread stale information through more interactions and require more frequent synchronization. The staleness bound is therefore a workload decision, not a universal switch.
