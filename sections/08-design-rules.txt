## Five checks guide system design

The model translates the measurements into five design checks.

[[FIG:levers]]

1. **Test residency first.** Calculate the coupling footprint from graph size, degree, index width, and coefficient width, and compare it with the usable on-chip tier.
2. **Treat synchronization as a quality parameter.** Sweep the synchronization interval and report time-to-solution rather than update rate alone.
3. **Size the device and link together.** After staging, the complete packet transfer must fit within the interior-computation window. Both link bandwidth and latency enter this test.
4. **Match partition geometry to the graph.** As device count rises, a compact mesh preserves interior locality longer than row slabs.
5. **Compare schedules at equal solution quality.** Faster sweeps do not help when success probability falls faster.

> The best distributed Ising accelerator is not simply the device with the fastest spin update. It is the design that keeps data resident and makes coordination invisible at the target graph size.
