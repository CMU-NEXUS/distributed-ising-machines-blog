## One equation locates the bottleneck

The paper decomposes sweep time into four costs. Compute and memory compete inside each serialized wave. Synchronization adds a barrier cost within a device. Communication adds the time required to move boundary state between devices.

[[FIG:sweep_model]]

This decomposition applies to dynamically scheduled GPUs and statically scheduled FPGAs or ASICs. The constants differ, but the structure does not. GPU memory service depends on cache residency, achieved bandwidth, and enough resident threads to cover latency. A streaming platform instead exposes synthesis-time constants: processing elements, clock frequency, initiation interval, and pipeline drain.

The model is calibrated once per GPU and then tested at block sizes that calibration never sees. It tracks the measured crossovers because its parameters correspond to physical mechanisms rather than a curve fitted separately at every operating point.

[[FIG:model_accuracy]]

That distinction matters for design. A measurement says what happened at one point. A calibrated term says what will change when the problem grows, the graph degree changes, or another device is added.
